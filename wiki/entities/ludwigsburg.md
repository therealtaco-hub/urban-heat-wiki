# Ludwigsburg

A mid-sized German city (~93,000 inhabitants) in the Stuttgart metropolitan region, and the closest case-study-city analog to Würzburg found in the vulnerability literature reviewed for this project.

## Overview
Part of the Stuttgart region (~2.8M inhabitants), facing above-German-average summer heat exposure. Selected by the ZURES research project (funded by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research) as a representative "growing medium-sized city" case study — the same city-size class as Würzburg, distinct from the megacity case studies (Yangtze River Delta, Singapore, Washington D.C.) that dominate the rest of the international heat-risk literature reviewed here.

## Key Data / Stats
- Population: ~93,000; part of Stuttgart metro region (~2.8M).
- Elderly share (65+): ~20% (2017 baseline) → ~30% projected by 2031 in the paper's own baseline scenario (higher than the official StaLaBW state-office projection of 28% by 2030).
- Ward-level welfare-receipt (SGB II) rates: ~5–11% baseline, varying significantly by district.

## Role in Urban Heat Research
Ludwigsburg is the subject of [[wiki/sources/birkmann-2021-ludwigsburg-vulnerability-scenarios]] (Birkmann et al. 2021), which builds ward-level future vulnerability scenarios (age + welfare-receipt, combined with planned housing-development storylines) and combines them with independently modeled local heat-stress projections (RCP 2.6/8.5). A companion ZURES paper (Laranjeira et al., household survey — referenced but not yet independently ingested into this wiki) provides empirical grounding for the age/poverty vulnerability indicator choice via a Ludwigsburg household survey.

## Connections
- Closest German mid-sized-city precedent for Würzburg's own HVI approach — used in this project's paper as the primary Cluster 2 (vulnerability) comparison.
- Research group: Institute of Spatial and Regional Planning (IREUS), University of Stuttgart (Jörn Birkmann et al.); GEO-Net (Hannover) provided the local climate modeling.
