# Manchester

UK city and home of the University of Manchester research group (Sarah Lindley and collaborators) responsible for the closest prior interactive climate-adaptation tool found in the literature review for this project — the GRaBS Assessment Tool and STAR Tools.

## Overview
Greater Manchester was the original case-study/development area for a lineage of GIS-based climate-risk decision-support tools spanning at least 2009–2014: an early hazard+vulnerability+adaptation-options tool ([[wiki/sources/smith-2009-manchester-gis-dss]]), later generalized and deployed across 10 European municipalities as the EU GRaBS project's Assessment Tool and STAR Tools ([[wiki/sources/cavan-2014-grabs-star-tools]]).

## Key Data / Stats
- Not directly relevant to this project's Würzburg heat/vulnerability numbers — Manchester's role here is as the origin city of the closest comparable *tool*, not as a case-study city for coefficients.
- STAR Tools usage stats (as of Dec 2013): ~1,100 unique visitors, ~200 completed sessions, 64% UK-origin traffic — useful context on real-world tool adoption for an EU-funded climate-adaptation tool.

## Role in Urban Heat Research
Not a source of cooling/vulnerability coefficients for this project — instead, the origin point of the tool-architecture lineage this project's paper positions itself against in Cluster 4 (interactive tools/DSS). Both papers explicitly discuss the tool's limitations (pre-calculated/screening-only, separate heat and runoff tools) that motivate this project's own integrated, live-simulation design.
