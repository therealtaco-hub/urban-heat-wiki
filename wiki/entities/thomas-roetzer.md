# Thomas Rötzer

Forest scientist (*Forstwissenschaftler*) at [[wiki/entities/tu-muenchen]], known for developing methodology to quantify the cooling output of urban trees and for co-authoring key allometric studies on urban tree crown growth in South Germany.

## Overview

Rötzer's work spans tree-level cooling quantification (kWh/year from height, crown diameter, leaf density, shadow cast), co-authorship on the key LST–tree canopy regression study for Munich, and allometric growth studies providing the empirical basis for crown-size modeling of urban trees in Central Europe. He is the single most connected researcher to this project.

## Role in Urban Heat Research

- Developed the tree cooling quantification methodology (kWh/year cooling per tree).
- Served as dissertation committee **chair** for Stratopoulos-Le Chalony's "Klimabäume" study on drought vs. cooling tradeoffs. *(Source: [[wiki/sources/klimabaeume-fuer-die-stadt]])*
- **Co-author** on García de León et al. — the Munich LST+tree canopy study conducted by Würzburg/DLR researchers. *(Source: [[wiki/sources/garcia-de-leon-lst-trees-munich]])*
- **Co-author** on Pretzsch et al. 2015 — the worldwide crown allometry study classifying 22 urban tree species into 5 allometric types; all four main Würzburg Baumkataster species fall in Type 1 (largest crown class). *(Source: [[wiki/sources/pretzsch-2015-urban-tree-crown-allometry]])*
- **Co-author** on Moser-Reischl et al. 2021 — the South German study including Würzburg field measurements directly, providing species-specific CPA–DBH regression tables for Tilia, Platanus, Robinia, Aesculus. *(Source: [[wiki/sources/moser-reischl-2021-urban-tree-growth-germany]])*
- Now at School of Life Sciences, TU München (Freising campus).
